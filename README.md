# Brief to Result

**Less prompting. More finished work.**

[English](README.md) | [Bahasa Indonesia](README.id.md) · [MIT License](LICENSE)

**Turn an everyday request into something useful:** affiliate hooks worth developing, customer replies ready to review, content drafts, organized meeting notes, study exercises, or a feature brief your team can work from.

BTR is a reusable skill for a compatible AI agent, not a separate chatbot or a new model. It gives your agent **80 focused workflows, Hook Lab with 24 creative formulas, three Outcome Packs, optional Project Profiles, and Repair Mode**. Describe the result you need; you do not have to learn the names of those features.

[Find your use case](#use-cases) · [How to use it](#quick-start) · [Features](#features) · [Installation](#installation) · [Local tools](#local-tools)

## What is it for?

The goal is to reduce the work between **“help me with this”** and a result you can use. BTR is designed to help the agent turn a short brief into a relevant output, respect your language and format, keep related drafts consistent, and revise only the parts you want changed.

It works from the context and material you provide. When essential information is missing, it should ask a focused question instead of inventing a price, deadline, product feature, or personal experience. These are working methods, not guarantees of perfect answers, virality, sales, or measured time savings.

> **Results first.** Ask for a better message and get the revised message—not a longer prompt. Prompt templates are produced only when requested. Preparing a draft does not authorize sending or publishing it.

<a id="quick-start"></a>
## Just mention @btr

After installing and selecting the skill in a compatible host, talk normally:

```text
@btr write 12 affiliate hooks for this product. Make the angles genuinely different.
```

```text
Help me reply to this customer calmly and clearly @btr.
```

```text
@btr only improve the opening. Keep the rest exactly as it is.
```

Paste or attach the relevant product details, message, notes, or file. BTR uses the context already available; you do not need to repeat the brief on every follow-up. With no task or relevant context, it asks one short question. Use `@btr menu` when you actually want options.

**The mention is not an installer.** Native selection depends on the host application. Use its installed-skill picker where required; clients with dollar-prefixed skill invocation use `$btr`. See the [invocation guide](brief-to-result/references/invocation.md). The skill does not add browsing, image generation, file access, or publishing permissions to an agent that lacks them.

<a id="affiliates"></a>
### Examples for affiliate creators

**Use it for:** finding a selling angle, opening a product video, showing a useful demo, and improving hooks without rewriting the entire script.

**Useful material:** what the product does, who it is for, supported specifications, any current offer, and your actual experience if you want a first-person review. A product link or photo helps when your agent can inspect it. You do not need every detail to start.

**1. Find different hook angles**

```text
@btr write 12 affiliate hooks for a desk cable clip that holds a cable end
at the edge of a desk. Target people who work at a desk. Use conversational
English, a little humor, and genuinely different everyday situations.
No invented price, popularity, or personal review. Return the hooks only.
```

**2. Turn the hooks into shootable video concepts**

```text
@btr choose three promising hooks from the previous batch.
For each, write a short video script with the opening line, first shot,
product demonstration, and one CTA. Aim for about 20 seconds;
treat the timing as an estimate until read aloud. I may earn a commission,
so include a clear disclosure in the script.
```

**3. Fix generic openings without changing the rest**

```text
@btr these hooks still sound like generic ads. Rewrite only the hooks
using more specific everyday moments. Keep the product facts,
script body, and CTA unchanged. Do not turn them into fake testimonials.
```

**What you get:** different reasons to notice the product, a clear connection between the opening and the demo, and revisions that stay within scope.

**Illustration, not a measured result:** for the fictional cable-clip brief above, a generic “This little gadget will change your life” might become **“The desk looks great. Finding the charging cable still involves crawling.”** The next shot would need to show the cable problem and the clip's actual function—not an unrelated reveal.

BTR's [Hook Lab](brief-to-result/references/hook-lab.md) develops new hooks from product-specific situations; it does not merely replace the product name in a list of famous openings. Its [24 formulas](brief-to-result/references/hook-formulas.md) cover friction, contrast, demonstration, buying decisions, genuine experience, and participation. Unsupported urgency or performance claims are replaced with another angle, not invented. See [more examples](brief-to-result/references/hook-examples.md).

<a id="sellers"></a>
### Examples for sellers and small businesses

**Use it for:** explaining why a product matters, answering buying objections, and announcing an offer consistently.

**Useful material:** your product description, target customer, actual price and conditions, or the customer's message.

**1. Turn specifications into useful product copy**

```text
@btr rewrite this product description for first-time buyers.
Explain which everyday problem each important feature helps with.
Keep every specification, price, and limitation accurate.
Return one clear description, not a list of writing tips.
```

**2. Reply when a customer says “too expensive”**

```text
@btr draft a short, friendly reply to this customer who says it is too expensive.
Use the benefits and terms in the product information I supplied.
Do not invent a discount or pressure them to buy.
```

**3. Prepare a small launch package**

```text
@btr turn these confirmed release notes and offer terms into one customer email,
two social posts, and five FAQ answers. Keep the facts consistent across them.
Prepare drafts only; do not publish anything.
```

**What you get:** buyer-oriented copy and coordinated drafts grounded in the same product information.

<a id="creators"></a>
### Examples for creators and social media managers

**Use it for:** finding an angle, turning source material into channel-ready drafts, and keeping a recognizable voice.

**Useful material:** a topic, article, transcript, existing post, or examples of your writing.

**1. Turn one source into a short post**

```text
@btr turn this article into two short English posts for X.
Open with the most interesting supported point. Keep my conversational voice
and the source's important caveats. Do not make the hook promise more than the posts explain.
```

**2. Build a carousel people can follow**

```text
@btr turn these notes into a six-slide carousel for beginners.
Give each slide one main idea, short on-slide copy, and a visual direction.
Make the first slide specific, not “Everything you need to know.”
```

**3. Repurpose a video without making identical posts**

```text
@btr use this transcript to create one LinkedIn post, one Instagram caption,
and one short-video outline. Keep the facts the same but adapt the angle
and structure to each channel. Return drafts, not a posting schedule.
```

**What you get:** distinct drafts adapted to their channel. A visual direction or outline is not an image or rendered video; actual production requires the relevant tools and a request to create it.

<a id="marketing"></a>
### Examples for marketing and copywriting

**Use it for:** testing different reasons to care, sharpening a value proposition, and designing fair creative comparisons.

**Useful material:** the audience, offer, product evidence, landing page, and relevant campaign constraints.

**1. Find more than one way to sell the same offer**

```text
@btr develop five ad angles for this offer and audience.
Make each address a different problem, objection, or buying motivation.
Give each angle one headline and one opening sentence. No unsupported superlatives.
```

**2. Make a landing-page opening easier to understand**

```text
@btr rewrite only this landing page's hero section.
Make clear who the product is for, what it helps them do, and the next step.
Preserve the actual offer and claims. Give me one recommended version.
```

**3. Plan an opening-line test**

```text
@btr propose an A/B test for these two opening lines.
Keep the audience, offer, body, and CTA comparable. Explain the hypothesis,
what to measure, and what the test cannot tell us. Do not predict a winning CTR.
```

**What you get:** differentiated concepts and a testable rationale—not a fabricated performance score.

<a id="freelancers"></a>
### Examples for freelancers and agencies

**Use it for:** turning vague client requests into workable scope and communicating boundaries without sounding hostile.

**Useful material:** the client brief, agreed deliverables, timeline, fees, and revision terms that already exist.

**1. Turn a brief into a proposal draft**

```text
@btr turn this client brief into a concise proposal with the objective,
deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, and open questions.
Use only the fee and timeline we have actually agreed. Mark anything unresolved.
```

**2. Respond to work outside the agreed scope**

```text
@btr help me reply to this additional request warmly but firmly.
Explain which parts are outside the attached scope and what needs agreement
before I proceed. Do not promise free work or invent a new fee.
```

**3. Apply feedback without undoing approved work**

```text
@btr apply this client's feedback only to the headline and closing paragraph.
Keep the approved body copy, product facts, and deliverables unchanged.
Return the revised draft and a short summary of what changed.
```

**What you get:** a clearer handoff, a ready-to-review client reply, or a scoped revision. Drafting a proposal does not send or accept an agreement.

<a id="support"></a>
### Examples for customer support and admin

**Use it for:** replying empathetically, explaining next steps, and turning repeated questions into usable guidance.

**Useful material:** an anonymized customer message, relevant service policy, known incident facts, or approved support notes.

**1. Answer a complaint without overpromising**

```text
@btr draft a calm reply to this complaint using the attached support policy.
Acknowledge the issue, explain the next supported step, and ask only for
information needed to help. Do not invent a refund or resolution time.
```

**2. Build an FAQ from recurring questions**

```text
@btr group these anonymized customer questions into an FAQ.
Use our supplied documentation for the answers. Flag questions the documentation
does not answer instead of filling in policy gaps.
```

**3. Prepare an internal escalation note**

```text
@btr turn this support thread into an internal handoff:
reported problem, observed evidence, steps already tried, customer impact,
and information still needed. Separate confirmed facts from suspected causes.
```

**What you get:** reviewable replies and concise internal notes. Remove unnecessary personal information before sharing source material; nothing is sent automatically.

<a id="teams"></a>
### Examples for work and team management

**Use it for:** making meetings actionable, choosing realistic priorities, and documenting repeatable work.

**Useful material:** meeting notes, a task list with deadlines and capacity, or a description of the current process.

**1. Turn meeting notes into actions**

```text
@btr turn these meeting notes into decisions, tasks, owners, and deadlines.
Mark missing owners or dates as not agreed. Keep open questions separate.
Do not assign people or commitments that were not in the notes.
```

**2. Decide what fits this week**

```text
@btr here are my tasks and deadlines. I have six hours available this week.
Recommend three priorities, what to defer, and the main tradeoff.
Use estimated effort transparently. Do not change my calendar.
```

**3. Write an SOP someone else can follow**

```text
@btr turn this process description into an SOP for a new team member.
Include when to start, required inputs, steps, exceptions, escalation,
and how to know the task is finished. Flag missing policy decisions.
```

**What you get:** a plan or procedure that makes the next action clear without inventing decisions for the team.

<a id="learning"></a>
### Examples for students and educators

**Use it for:** understanding difficult material, practicing recall, and planning a lesson around a clear objective.

**Useful material:** your notes, a chapter excerpt, learning objective, learner level, or your own attempt at a question.

**1. Understand a confusing topic**

```text
@btr explain this part of my notes for a beginner.
Start with the main idea, give one concrete example, then clarify the most
likely misconception. Keep technical details accurate.
```

**2. Practice before seeing the answers**

```text
@btr create five practice questions from these notes, from easy to harder.
Give me the questions first and wait for my answers before showing explanations.
Do not introduce material that is not covered without labeling it as extra.
```

**3. Prepare a focused lesson**

```text
@btr use this material to draft a 30-minute lesson for the stated learner level.
Include one learning objective, an explanation, an activity, and a check
for understanding. Treat the timing as a plan, not a measured duration.
```

**What you get:** explanations, exercises, or a teaching plan grounded in the supplied material—not invented quotations or citations.

<a id="product"></a>
### Examples for founders, product teams, and developers

**Use it for:** challenging an idea, defining behavior before building, and debugging from evidence.

**Useful material:** the problem and audience, feature notes, existing flows, code, logs, or a reproducible error.

**1. Test an idea before building too much**

```text
@btr challenge this product idea. Identify the three riskiest assumptions,
what evidence would support or reject each, and a small experiment to run first.
Do not treat market demand as proven.
```

**2. Turn a feature idea into a buildable handoff**

```text
@btr turn this feature idea into a brief, user flow, acceptance criteria,
edge cases, and dependency-ordered implementation tasks.
Flag decisions we still need to make. Do not start coding yet.
```

**3. Diagnose a bug before proposing a patch**

```text
@btr inspect this error, the relevant code, and the steps to reproduce it.
Separate observed facts from hypotheses. Recommend the smallest useful check
and, when the evidence supports it, a focused fix and a way to test it.
```

**What you get:** an experiment plan, implementation specification, or evidence-led diagnosis. A proposed fix is not a tested fix; BTR must distinguish the two.

<a id="everyday"></a>
### Examples for everyday life

**Use it for:** expressing yourself clearly, making practical choices, and turning scattered notes into a manageable plan.

**Useful material:** the message you need to answer, your constraints, or the options you are considering.

**1. Decline an invitation politely**

```text
@btr help me decline this invitation warmly in two sentences.
Do not invent an excuse or suggest a replacement date I have not agreed to.
```

**2. Organize a weekend task list**

```text
@btr organize these household tasks around the four hours I have on Saturday.
Group related tasks, leave a little buffer, and explain what to postpone
if they do not all fit. Do not schedule anything in an app.
```

**3. Compare options using what matters to you**

```text
@btr compare these three options against my budget and priorities.
Recommend one, explain the tradeoff, and say what missing information
could change the choice. Use the facts I supplied, not invented specifications.
```

**What you get:** a usable draft, a realistic proposed plan, or a comparison with its uncertainty made clear.

## What powers these examples?

You do not need to select a feature manually. BTR routes the request according to the result you need.

| Feature | What it adds | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hook Lab** | 24 creative formulas for sales and affiliate openings, diversity checks, and a hook-to-demo connection | [Hook Lab](brief-to-result/references/hook-lab.md) |
| **Outcome Packs** | Related deliverables built from one shared set of facts | [Pack protocol](brief-to-result/references/outcome-packs.md) |
| **Project Profiles** | Explicitly selected local preferences and sourced project facts | [Profile guide](brief-to-result/references/project-profiles.md) |
| **Repair Mode** | Targeted changes with protected wording and preserved surrounding content | [Repair guide](brief-to-result/references/repair-mode.md) |
| **80 workflows** | Writing, understanding, ideas, sales, content, product, technical work, and operations | [Skill entry point](brief-to-result/SKILL.md) |

The 24 hook formulas supplement the 80 workflows; they are not 24 additional general workflows. Their creative usefulness still needs real-world evaluation.

### Three Outcome Packs

| Pack | Default deliverables |
| --- | --- |
| [Launch Kit](brief-to-result/packs/launch-kit.md) | Main message, email, social drafts, FAQ, launch checklist, sources and open items |
| [Idea to Build](brief-to-result/packs/idea-to-build.md) | Brief, user flow, acceptance criteria, implementation tasks, open decisions |
| [Research to Content](brief-to-result/packs/research-to-content.md) | Source map, synthesis, angles, content draft, verification notes |

Your requested scope overrides the defaults. One email does not become a full campaign. Packs are followed by the host agent; the Python helper does not generate content or call a model.

### Reuse project context and revise precisely

```text
@btr use the project profile .btr/profiles/northstar.json for this announcement.
```

Profiles are optional and explicitly selected, not hidden memory or automatic cross-chat persistence. Current task instructions override preferences. Product facts retain their sources and review dates; excluded or stale facts must not silently fill gaps. A user-maintained `confirmed` label is not independent verification. Local files are not encrypted and their contents can be seen by the model when you ask the agent to read them. Do not store credentials or private customer records.

```text
@btr shorten only the second paragraph. Keep the price, conditions, and CTA unchanged.
```

Repair Mode guides the agent's revision. Its optional UTF-8 text helper can check source hashes, exact wording locks, and edit boundaries, preview a diff, and write to a new file only. Meaning and style still require review; a prompt alone is not deterministic enforcement.

## Installation

### Build the short-name btr package

The canonical sources live in `brief-to-result/`. The builder creates a complete, standalone skill named `btr`, including its runtime resources and MIT license.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/makanSukros/brief-to-result.git
cd brief-to-result
python3 tools/build_btr.py --output dist/btr.zip
```

Import `dist/btr.zip` using your compatible host's supported skill-installation process, then enable/select BTR. See the [invocation guide](brief-to-result/references/invocation.md) for host-specific instructions. Publishing to GitHub or typing `@btr` does not install it in an account.

For a first local installation in an agent that supports `~/.agents/skills/`:

```bash
python3 tools/build_btr.py --directory "$HOME/.agents/skills/btr"
```

The builder refuses existing destinations. Review/back up an old installation before replacing it; use your client's documented directory when different. Prefer one active installation to avoid duplicate entries. Keep project profiles outside the installed skill directory.

Existing `brief-to-result/` installations remain supported. Copying the canonical directory installs the long name, not `btr`; do not only rename its folder because the directory and frontmatter must match. Use the builder for the short name. See [invocation details](brief-to-result/references/invocation.md) for long-name selection and alias limits.

Markdown instructions can be followed without Python. The builder and `btr.py` require **Python 3.10+**, use the standard library, and make no network/model calls. A compatible host agent and its own model access are still required.

## Optional local tools

Ordinary writing, hook, and planning requests do not require running these commands. The commands below run from the cloned repository root; for an installed copy, substitute its actual path for `brief-to-result/`.

<details>
<summary><strong>Browse packs and workflows</strong></summary>

```bash
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py packs list
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py packs show launch-kit
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/lookup.py --list
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/lookup.py --id W03
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/lookup.py --query "caption"
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/text_metrics.py --help
```

Pack commands show instructions, not generated deliverables. `--json` returns machine-readable pack metadata.

| Area | IDs |
| --- | --- |
| Writing and editing | W01–W10 |
| Understanding and learning | U01–U10 |
| Ideation and strategy | I01–I10 |
| Sales and persuasion | M01–M10 |
| Content and storytelling | C01–C10 |
| Product and UX | P01–P10 |
| Technical work | T01–T10 |
| Operations and decisions | O01–O10 |

Each workflow contains a trigger, input, method, output, checks, and example.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Create and check a project profile</strong></summary>

```bash
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py profiles init northstar
# Edit .btr/profiles/northstar.json with your real preferences and supported facts.
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py profiles check .btr/profiles/northstar.json
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py profiles context .btr/profiles/northstar.json
```

Initialization refuses to overwrite a profile and creates a local ignore file if absent. Ignore rules do not protect already-tracked files; review `git status` before committing. Add `--as-of YYYY-MM-DD` for reproducible date checks or `--strict` to `profiles check` to fail when any fact is excluded. See the [template](brief-to-result/assets/profile.template.json) and [schema rules](brief-to-result/references/project-profiles.md).

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Preview and apply a scoped text repair</strong></summary>

The included Northstar example is fictional. Create a plan for its first line:

```bash
mkdir -p .btr
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py repair plan examples/repair-before.txt \
  --allow 1:1 --keep "Try the demo." --output .btr/repair.json
```

Edit only the `replacement` string in `.btr/repair.json`, retaining its trailing `\n`. For example: `Export your report without retyping it.\n`. Then:

```bash
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py repair preview examples/repair-before.txt --plan .btr/repair.json
python3 brief-to-result/scripts/btr.py repair apply examples/repair-before.txt \
  --plan .btr/repair.json --output .btr/revised.txt
```

Repeat `--allow START:END` for disjoint original line ranges and `--keep` for additional exact wording locks. Preview writes nothing; apply refuses existing destinations. The helper rejects stale plans and invalid or overlapping ranges. UTF-8 text only, not binary document editing. Plans contain source excerpts; handle them as confidentially as the source. Scope fields are a reviewable contract, not a security sandbox.

</details>

## Language, evidence, and limits

English is the base language for the skill and this README; the [Indonesian README](README.id.md) provides an equally usable starting point. Deliverables follow the requested language and established audience context. Existing text keeps its language unless translation is requested. English does not imply a US audience, USD pricing, or a particular time zone.

Examples are adaptable requests and illustrations, not testimonials or model benchmark results. Review important claims before publishing. Affiliate experience, offers, pricing, scarcity, and comparisons need an actual basis. BTR should change an unsupported angle rather than invent proof. Disclosure must match the actual commercial relationship; the skill is not a worldwide legal-compliance certification.

Browsing, media generation, integrations, and file operations depend on the host's available tools and authorization. No draft is automatically sent, no ad budget is spent, and no background job is created just because a workflow mentions it.

## Tests

```bash
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

Tests cover local helpers, packaging, error paths, profile freshness, repair boundaries, catalog structure, and documentation resources. See [manual acceptance examples](examples/README.md) and [Hook Lab cases](brief-to-result/references/hook-examples.md). Passing local tests does not prove creativity, truth, identical behavior across agents, or conversion lift.

Helper exit codes: `0` success, `1` excluded facts under a strict profile check, `2` invalid input or write failure. `text_metrics.py` uses `1` for a failed length check. If a repair source changes, create a new plan; the original file is never overwritten by the repair helper.
