# Writing and editing

Use these recipes to improve existing text. Read only the relevant recipe. Preserve the user's meaning, facts, and constraints; requested length and variant counts override defaults. Follow the main skill's language policy: preserve the source language for edits unless translation or a different target language is requested. Explicit output or audience-language requirements take precedence; use English for new content without a language signal. If the source is missing, request it rather than inventing a replacement and presenting it as a revision.

## W01 — Make the main point clear in five seconds

- **Trigger:** “Make this clearer,” “get to the point,” or a message whose main point is buried under a long introduction.
- **Input:** Required: source text. Optional: audience, channel, desired action, and length limit; reuse context already available.
- **Method:** Identify the central message and the reader's immediate need. Put the main point first, use concrete subjects and verbs, then arrange supporting details by importance. Separate required actions from background information.
- **Output:** One finished version that quickly explains what happened, why it matters, and what to do when action is needed.
- **Check:** Ensure the opening remains accurate on its own. Five seconds is a clarity goal, not a measured reading result. Preserve important conditions.
- **Example:** “Rewrite this announcement so employees immediately understand the schedule change and what they need to do.”

## W02 — Offer three distinct tones and a recommendation

- **Trigger:** “Make this sound better,” “give me a few versions,” or a request to choose how to communicate a message.
- **Input:** Required: source text or intended message. Optional: relationship with the reader, purpose, formality, and number of variants.
- **Method:** Preserve the information while developing three approaches: clear and neutral; persuasive with supporting reasons; concise and direct. Change structure, emphasis, and rhythm meaningfully. Select the version that best fits the reader and purpose.
- **Output:** Three labeled versions and a brief recommendation with a practical reason. Follow any requested tones or variant count instead.
- **Check:** Make the versions meaningfully different. Do not add unsupported benefits, promises, pressure, or familiarity.
- **Example:** “Give me casual, professional, and direct versions of this introduction; recommend one for a new business contact.”

## W03 — Shorten without losing meaning

- **Trigger:** “Make it shorter,” “cut this by half,” “maximum 400 characters,” or a strict space constraint.
- **Input:** Required: source text. Optional: character or word limit, content that must remain, and destination.
- **Method:** Mark essential facts, conditions, names, links, and actions. Remove repetition and empty openings, combine sentences, and choose concise wording. Count the final text with an available counter and revise until it fits. Base percentage reductions on the source length; prioritize any absolute limit.
- **Output:** A finished version within the requested limit, with essential meaning intact.
- **Check:** Never claim an exact count without counting. Include spaces, punctuation, hashtags, and links when they belong to the final text. Preserve material exceptions.
- **Example:** “Reduce this to 250 characters, keeping the price, link, and eligibility conditions.”

## W04 — Preserve the writer's voice

- **Trigger:** “Keep my style,” “make it sound less artificial,” “lightly polish this,” or a request for an authentic revision.
- **Input:** Required: draft. Optional: writing samples, preferred or avoided words, and recipient context.
- **Method:** Identify forms of address, formality, sentence length, humor, and recurring expressions from the supplied material. Fix confusing wording, structure, and mechanical errors without replacing the writer's personality. Preserve useful voice characteristics; do not infer a personality unsupported by the samples.
- **Output:** One polished revision that retains the writer's voice. Explain changes only when requested or when a material change in meaning needs attention.
- **Check:** Avoid unnecessary corporate language, exaggerated slang, or invented familiarity. Do not treat every typo as an essential stylistic choice.
- **Example:** “Polish this founder update, keeping the conversational voice and dry humor.”

## W05 — Make the message professional and firm

- **Trigger:** “Make this more authoritative,” “firm but polite,” or a workplace message needing clear boundaries and action.
- **Input:** Required: intended message and purpose. Optional: recipient, working relationship, applicable rules, deadline, and consequences already authorized.
- **Method:** State the issue or requirement specifically. Identify the requested action, responsible person when known, and supplied deadline. Add a concise reason when it helps the reader respond. Remove sarcasm, accusations about motives, and repeated apologies.
- **Output:** A ready-to-use message with a controlled tone, clear request, and closing appropriate to the relationship.
- **Check:** Do not invent penalties, legal grounds, authority, or deadlines. Distinguish requests, policies, and decisions. Use verifiable statements when a dispute is unresolved.
- **Example:** “Make this reporting policy firm and respectful: every bug report must use the template and the designated channel.”

## W06 — Translate and localize naturally

- **Trigger:** “Translate this naturally,” “localize this,” or a literal translation that sounds awkward in its intended setting.
- **Input:** Required: source text. Optional: target language, region, audience, channel, and tone. Honor explicit target and audience-language requirements; otherwise use English.
- **Method:** Understand the meaning and relationship before translating. Replace literal idioms and sentence structures with natural equivalents. Preserve names, numbers, links, uncertainty, and commitments. Adapt forms of address to the context without assuming a specific locale.
- **Output:** One usable translation. Offer alternatives only for meaningful ambiguity or requested tone variations.
- **Check:** Do not add friendliness, promises, superlatives, or claims. If the source is already English and the intended translation direction blocks progress, clarify it briefly.
- **Example:** “Translate this introduction into natural English: professional, warm, and suitable for a first business conversation.”

## W07 — Organize a messy draft

- **Trigger:** “Reorganize this,” “these notes are scattered,” or mixed notes that need to become a coherent message or document.
- **Input:** Required: notes or draft. Optional: output type, audience, mandatory order, and length limit.
- **Method:** Group related ideas, merge duplicates, and distinguish facts, reasons, decisions, and actions. Choose an order that serves the reader: importance, chronology, or problem followed by solution. Preserve essential details and flag contradictions that the source cannot resolve.
- **Output:** A complete draft using headings, paragraphs, or lists as needed. Keep genuinely blocking questions separate from the usable text.
- **Check:** Do not fill logical gaps with invented facts. Ensure the new order preserves causality and does not turn a proposal into an approved decision.
- **Example:** “Turn these scattered meeting notes into an internal update: decisions first, then owners, tasks, and deadlines.”

## W08 — Critique and deliver a revised version

- **Trigger:** “What's wrong with this?”, “critique this draft,” or a request for both diagnosis and improved text.
- **Input:** Required: draft. Optional: goal, audience, channel, success criteria, and protected sections.
- **Method:** Assess alignment with the goal, clarity, structure, evidence, and the reader's next action. Identify up to three weaknesses with the greatest effect, point to concrete passages, and revise the text to address those weaknesses directly.
- **Output:** A concise diagnosis followed by one finished version. If the user requests only the result, provide the revision without a lengthy critique.
- **Check:** Do not manufacture three problems when only one matters. Connect criticism to the goal rather than personal taste. Preserve effective passages.
- **Example:** “Critique this proposal from the perspective of a busy buyer, then write a stronger version.”

## W09 — Replace jargon with plain language

- **Trigger:** “Make this understandable to non-experts,” “this is too technical,” or specialist text intended for a general audience.
- **Input:** Required: source text. Optional: audience, required terminology, level of detail, and intended use.
- **Method:** Explain technical terms through their function or effect on the reader. Retain necessary terminology with a short explanation on first use. Use direct sentences and examples grounded in the source. Focus on rewriting the text rather than adding an extensive lesson.
- **Output:** Replacement copy for a message, help page, or interface. Include steps only when the source describes an action or the task calls for them.
- **Check:** Preserve accuracy and uncertainty. Avoid guarantees and analogies more confusing than the original terminology.
- **Example:** “Rewrite this session-expired message for everyday users and explain what they should do next.”

## W10 — Reply appropriately to the conversation

- **Trigger:** “What should I reply?”, “respond professionally,” or a supplied message needing an appropriate response.
- **Input:** Required: the latest message and visible context. Optional: relationship goal, earlier conversation, language, and tone.
- **Method:** Determine whether the message asks a question, acknowledges something, declines, postpones, or closes the exchange. Address the actual need and match the reply's length to the conversation. If no response is necessary, say so briefly and offer a short acknowledgment only when useful.
- **Output:** One ready-to-copy response or a recommendation to leave the exchange closed. Do not automatically add questions or promotional content.
- **Check:** Do not invent familiarity, agreements, schedules, or another person's status. Sending a reply requires appropriate authorization.
- **Example:** “They only replied ‘Sounds good’ after my introduction. Should I respond? Keep any reply brief.”
