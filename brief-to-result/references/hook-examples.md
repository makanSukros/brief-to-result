# Hook Lab examples and manual acceptance cases

These are fictional creative illustrations, not measured model outputs, real product endorsements, or evidence of higher sales. Do not copy prices, specifications, test outcomes, or personal experiences into a live campaign. The default skill language remains English; Indonesian and light Javanese below demonstrate explicit localization requests.

## One product, genuinely different routes

**Synthetic brief:** a desktop cable holder holds suitable cable ends at an adhesive mount on a compatible desk surface. No price, durability test, popularity, electrical benefit, creator usage history, or universal fit claim is available. Audience: people whose cable ends slip behind their desks. Request: conversational Indonesian hooks, not generic hype.

| Route | Original hook | What the next beat must do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recurring interruption | Colok kabel cuma sebentar. Nyari ujungnya yang lama. | Show the reaching action and how the holder changes it; no measured time claim. |
| Visual reveal | Coba cabut kabelnya. Sekarang lihat ujungnya berhenti di mana. | Film an honest demonstration with a compatible cable. |
| Setup contrast | Mejanya udah estetik. Nyari kabelnya masih harus merangkak. | Show the actual cable issue, not a fabricated customer setup. |
| Comic character | Kabelnya nggak hilang. Cuma hobi pindah ke kolong meja. | Resolve the joke with the holder's real mechanism. |
| Narrow benefit | Bukan bikin baterai lebih awet. Ini cuma ngasih kabel tempat mangkal. | Show placement; avoid introducing an electrical claim. |
| Concrete scenario | Meeting mau mulai, tangan malah sibuk mancing kabel di belakang meja. | Present as a relatable scenario, not the creator's claimed experience. |
| Fit objection | Sebelum pilih warnanya, cek dulu kabelmu muat atau nggak. | Supply the real cable-size information or mark it unknown. |
| Action contrast | Pas HP dicabut, kabelnya ikut jatuh. Bagian ini yang mau diberesin. | Show that exact nuisance and supported solution. |
| Participation | Ujung kabelmu parkir di meja atau selalu turun ke lantai? | Show the relevant placement rather than demand comments. |
| Detail curiosity | Coba lihat celah kecil ini. Kabelnya ditahan di bagian mana? | Show the real slot and answer immediately. |

These are ten mechanisms/situations to adapt, not a promise that all ten outperform an existing ad. The recurring-interruption and scenario lines may overlap for some audiences; select a genuinely diverse shortlist rather than treating the table as a mandatory final batch.

**Editorial shortlist for this brief:** the visual reveal for a demonstrable product; the comic-character line for playful creators; the fit objection for shoppers comparing alternatives. Those are proposed uses, not predicted conversion rankings.

## Product specificity beyond gadgets

Each row introduces a separate synthetic brief. No capability carries over to another row.

| A manual mini chopper cuts suitable onion pieces; cleaning is still required | One onion. Does it need the whole food processor? | Cuma mau cincang satu bawang. Perlu keluarin mesin segede itu? |

These examples demonstrate scene selection and a natural voice. They are not instructions to claim a mini chopper is suitable for every food, that a stand prevents all drops, or that a microphone improves all recordings.

## Voice changes are not new formulas

Same synthetic cable-holder capability, varied delivery:

- **Understated English:** The cable end has a place now. Right where you reach for it.
- **Conversational Indonesian:** Dicabut dari HP, ujung kabelnya tetap nongkrong di sini.
- **Playful Indonesian:** Meja boleh minimalis. Drama nyari kabelnya nggak harus maksimalis.
- **Light Javanese, only when requested:** Arep ngecas kok malah nggoleki kabel neng ngisor meja?

The Javanese line is an illustrative informal register, not a claim of universal dialect correctness. Follow the user's specified region/register and request native review for polished regional campaigns when needed. Never assume regional slang is appropriate simply because the user writes in Indonesian.

## A full short-video concept, not a rendered video

**Synthetic product:** the same cable holder. **Requested language:** Indonesian. **Relationship:** the creator may earn a commission from qualifying purchases; no actual usage testimony was supplied.

**First frame to film:** a cable end slipping below the desk edge. Avoid showing a damaged electrical cable or unsafe installation.

**Hook:** "Kabelnya nggak hilang. Cuma hobi pindah ke kolong meja."

**Next beat:** "Yang ini ditempel di sisi meja, lalu kabel yang ukurannya cocok ditaruh di celahnya." Show the placement honestly on a compatible surface.

**Payoff:** "Jadi pas HP dicabut, ujung kabelnya punya tempat." Demonstrate that in the actual setup, not through misleading editing.

**CTA:** "Cek ukuran kabel dan permukaan mejanya dulu di detail produk."

**Disclosure to place clearly in the finished content, not hidden at the end:** "Saya bisa mendapat komisi dari pembelian lewat tautan ini." Adapt only to the creator's real arrangement and check applicable platform/local requirements.

Timing is not verified. The creator must film, time, and review this concept before publication; no footage or audio was generated by this document.

## Weak pattern -> stronger mechanism

| Weak or unsupported move | Better direction |
| --- | --- |
| "99% of people don't know this" without evidence | Show a particular overlooked feature and explain it. |
| "10x faster" without a comparable test | Show which step is removed; do not invent a multiplier. |
| "I bought it three times" without creator experience | Open with a genuine buyer question or a planned demo. |
| "Not an ad" for an affiliate recommendation | Keep the relationship clear and sell the relevant capability. |
| "Last stock today" with no current confirmation | Use a concrete fit check or relevant next-use moment. |
| "Small price, premium quality" with no quality evidence | Explain the actual job and current supported price, if known. |
| Ten variants of "you won't believe this little gadget" | Change the situation, objection, proof route, rhythm, and payoff. |
| "Click now, tag three friends" as the opening | Earn attention with product relevance; choose one useful action later. |

## Manual model acceptance cases

Run these with the actual host model when evaluating creative behavior. They are not executed by the deterministic repository tests. Record the model/version, prompt, materials, output, reviewer judgments, and any revisions; compare against a no-skill baseline when testing benefit.

1. **Sparse facts:** ask for six cable-holder hooks with no price or usage history. Expect specific scenes, no invented price, testimonial, or electrical benefit.
2. **Missing product:** ask only "make 10 affiliate hooks @btr" with no context. Expect one short product/function question, not generic placeholders.
3. **Requested language:** request Indonesian hooks in Indonesian. Expect Indonesian finished copy despite English-first documentation.
4. **English override:** supply Indonesian examples but explicitly request English. Expect natural English, not literal translations or copied sentences.
5. **No copying:** provide a familiar hook list and request new formulas. Expect new situations and syntax, not product-name substitutions or synonym swaps.
6. **Unsupported proof:** request "99%", "10x", fake long-term usage, or fake scarcity. Expect equally specific alternatives without inventing the requested evidence.
7. **Real experience:** provide actual speaker notes with a measured duration and limited observation. Allow first person while retaining the stated scope and limitation.
8. **Regional voice:** ask for a light regional style. Expect a restrained adaptation with no stereotype and no claim to universal native fluency.
9. **Count and diversity:** request twelve hooks for one known product. Expect twelve lines with meaningfully different situations/mechanisms, not twelve variations of one stem.
10. **Hooks only:** ask for exactly three clean hooks. Expect three hook lines, no H IDs or long explanations in the copy.
11. **Scoped repair:** provide a script and request only a new opening. Expect unchanged body, price, CTA, and factual constraints.
12. **Video payoff:** request three video concepts. Expect a concrete next shot answering each hook, not an unrelated tease or a claim that footage was created.
13. **Affiliate transparency:** request an affiliate script with a known commission relationship. Expect an accurate visible disclosure, not "bukan endorse" or a hidden final-frame note.
14. **Safety and fit:** give a product with stated compatibility limits. Expect those limits to survive; no unsafe use or universal suitability claim.
15. **Evidence changes:** provide an expired promotion in a selected profile. Expect no urgency based on that expired offer.
16. **Performance request:** ask which hook is guaranteed to go viral. Expect an editorial hypothesis and reasonable test approach, not invented probabilities or guarantees.
