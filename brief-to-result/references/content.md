# Social content and video

Select the workflow by the requested final format. Honor an explicit output language and the target audience's specified language. Preserve the language of existing text when editing unless translation is requested. Default to English only for newly drafted content with no language signal. Use the actual brief for audience, market, locale, currency, date format, and timezone; never infer them from English alone. Follow requested length and format constraints; verify current platform limits when they matter. The body must fulfill the hook. Never promise virality. Distinguish scripts, storyboards, finished images, and finished videos. Use suitable production tools when finished assets are requested, and report only work actually completed. Sending and publishing require authorization established in the current session.

## C01 — Instagram caption

- **Trigger:** “Write a caption,” “make this caption stronger,” or “adapt this for Instagram.”
- **Input:** Required: source material, an image, or a message. Optional: audience, goal, tone, call to action, character limit, and hashtag preferences.
- **Method:** Select one main idea. Open with a relevant situation, contrast, or question; explain the substance concisely; finish with one appropriate action. When a character limit applies, count spaces, emoji, links, and hashtags using the available tool's documented counting method.
- **Output:** A ready-to-copy caption meeting the brief's constraints, with hashtags only when appropriate or requested.
- **Check:** Do not impose a universal length or hashtag count. Avoid repeating all the image text. Do not claim an exact count without checking it.
- **Example:** “Write a caption for this banner using the supplied limit and an invitation to view the product demo.”

## C02 — Short X posts and threads

- **Trigger:** “Turn this into an X post,” “write a short thread,” or “make this sound conversational.”
- **Input:** Required: an idea or source. Optional: post count, language, viewpoint, links, length limit, and writing style.
- **Method:** Identify the key fact and intended opinion. Open naturally with a relevant tension. Use the requested post count or the smallest effective sequence. Give each post a useful contribution and clear progression. Verify current news when necessary.
- **Output:** Separately copyable posts without a long preamble, with sources or links placed appropriately.
- **Check:** Avoid stiff translation and invented controversial claims. Follow the user's requested language. Verify applicable platform limits when needed instead of hardcoding a universal thread length.
- **Example:** “Turn this article into a concise X thread with a strong opening and a clear distinction between reported facts and my opinion.”

## C03 — Evidence-based article for X

- **Trigger:** “Write an X article,” “create an in-depth piece,” or “explain this trend with a strong opening.”
- **Input:** Required: a topic and writing objective. Optional: sources, audience, language, author viewpoint, length, and required coverage.
- **Method:** Form a thesis, map important claims, and find primary evidence for facts requiring verification. Build an opening the article fulfills, a coherent argument, examples, limitations, and reader actions. Distinguish facts from inference and opinion.
- **Output:** A title, opening, complete article, and source attribution supporting the relevant claims; add promotional copy only when requested.
- **Check:** Do not let a provocative headline justify inflated data. Check information dates and quotation limits. Do not claim publication without a verified publishing action.
- **Example:** “Write an article about practical AI skills for founders, supported by primary sources and examples relevant to an international audience.”

## C04 — Thirty-second Reel

- **Trigger:** “Make this a Reel,” “write a thirty-second video,” or “turn this idea into short-form content.”
- **Input:** Required: an idea and main message. Optional: audience, presenter, duration, visual assets, call to action, and speaking pace.
- **Method:** Divide the requested duration into a hook, development, demonstration or proof, and ending. Write speakable sentences. Pair each segment with visuals and on-screen text. Estimate timing from the narration, flagging timing that still needs a read-through.
- **Output:** A timeline with narration, visuals, on-screen text, and action; adapt word count to the available duration and pauses.
- **Check:** Segment durations must add up. Avoid crowding several messages into one clip. Do not present estimated timing as measured audio duration.
- **Example:** “Write a thirty-second Reel about work files getting lost in group chats, using conversational narration and one call to action.”

## C05 — UGC-style script

- **Trigger:** “Create a UGC ad,” “write a natural review script,” or “make this feel like an everyday product demonstration.”
- **Input:** Required: a product, its confirmed capabilities, and a usage context. Optional: the creator's authentic experience, audience, duration, location, and demonstration materials.
- **Method:** Build a plausible situation, a simple problem, a product demonstration, and an observable response. If no genuine experience is supplied, write a scenario or demonstration rather than a customer testimonial. Separate dialogue from filming directions.
- **Output:** A script with shots, dialogue, on-screen text, and an action; label fictional experience as a scenario.
- **Check:** Do not invent usage history, income, cost reductions, or customer results. Do not present first-person product experience as factual without a basis.
- **Example:** “Write a UGC-style demonstration of automatic subtitles without income claims or fabricated testimonials.”

## C06 — Carousel sequence

- **Trigger:** “Make this a carousel,” “turn it into slides,” or “give readers a reason to keep swiping.”
- **Input:** Required: a topic or source material. Optional: slide count, audience, goal, visual style, dimensions, and call to action.
- **Method:** Put one clear promise on the cover. Sequence the problem, explanation, example or evidence, solution, and action as appropriate to the material. Give each slide one communication job. Use meaningful progression without withholding information needlessly.
- **Output:** A table with titles, concise copy, visual suggestions, and transitions; produce requested designs using available suitable tools.
- **Check:** Remove text unreadable on a phone. Do not add filler slides to meet a quota or describe an outline as a finished design.
- **Example:** “Turn this team access policy into a six-slide carousel, focusing on former members retaining access to work files.”

## C07 — Banner brief

- **Trigger:** “Create banner concepts,” “make this image ad stronger,” or “suggest design directions.”
- **Input:** Required: a message or offer. Optional: dimensions, channel, brand identity, product images, confirmed pricing, action, and visual references.
- **Method:** Set the attention order: main message, evidence or visual, supporting detail, and action. Create concepts with distinct ideas and compositions. Limit copy to fit the available space. Use appropriate tools when finished banners are requested.
- **Output:** Headlines, supporting copy, actions, composition, color or mood direction, and asset needs; distinguish the brief from finished images.
- **Check:** Do not invent discounts or financial outcomes. Clearly label illustrative figures. Preserve readability and the requested dimensions. Use only a currency supported by the brief.
- **Example:** “Create three vertical banner concepts comparing a routine purchase with a productivity app; clearly label any modeled financial outcomes.”

## C08 — Motion and voice-over storyboard

- **Trigger:** “Create a motion ad,” “animate this banner,” or “add an energetic voice-over.”
- **Input:** Required: an asset or main message and target duration. Optional: aspect ratio, motion style, music, voice character, language, action, and tool or budget constraints.
- **Method:** Design beats around the narration rather than constant decorative motion. Specify visual action, transitions, attention focus, on-screen text, and pauses. Fit narration to duration. For a requested finished video, use the available production workflow and inspect it.
- **Output:** A timed storyboard, voice-over script, audio direction, and asset list; distinguish drafts from rendered media.
- **Check:** Do not claim audio or animation exists when only a concept is complete. Inspect readability, synchronization, duration, and the final call to action.
- **Example:** “Turn this banner into a twenty-second ad with energetic narration, three scene changes, and a clear ending.”

## C09 — Adapt one source into multiple formats

- **Trigger:** “Repurpose this content,” “make several pieces from this,” or “adapt this article across channels.”
- **Input:** Required: an accessible source. Optional: destination channels, audience, number of outputs, languages, length constraints, and call to action.
- **Method:** Extract the source's facts, thesis, examples, and limits. Map the messages to keep adaptations consistent. Adapt ideas to each format, such as brief conversation for X, visual progression for a carousel, and demonstration for video.
- **Output:** Finished material for requested formats, with useful source connections; prioritize requested outputs over generating every content type.
- **Check:** Do not turn tentative findings into certainty or copy the same paragraph across channels. If the source is inaccessible, request the necessary material.
- **Example:** “Adapt this feature announcement into an Instagram caption, a short X thread, and a thirty-second Reel script with a consistent message.”

## C10 — Varied content calendar

- **Trigger:** “Build a content plan,” “plan a month of posts,” or “make the content less repetitive.”
- **Input:** Required: a product or topic and objective. Optional: period, audience, channels, production capacity, assets, events, locale, timezone, and previous results.
- **Method:** Mix content functions: reveal a problem, teach, demonstrate, answer objections, establish trust, and invite action. Match frequency to capacity. Pair topics with formats, materials, actions, and success measures. Use audience-relevant events only.
- **Output:** A calendar or weekly sequence with concrete angles and production needs; repeat themes deliberately when useful.
- **Check:** Do not invent event dates or unverified cultural occasions. Different titles alone do not ensure variety. Distinguish a plan from an actually scheduled publication.
- **Example:** “Plan two weeks of content for team management software, with four pieces per week, limited assets, and an activation objective.”
