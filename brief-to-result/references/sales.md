# Sales and campaigns

Use the latest confirmed offer details from the actual brief. Old prices, package limits, customer counts, results, testimonials, and schedules are not permanent facts. Honor an explicit output language and the target audience's specified language. Preserve the language of existing text when editing unless translation is requested. Default to English only for newly drafted content with no language signal. Use the actual brief for market, locale, currency, date format, and timezone; never infer them from English alone. Produce usable drafts; sending or publishing requires authorization established in the current session and appropriate tool capabilities.

## M01 — Problem, desired outcome, solution, action

- **Trigger:** “Make this more persuasive,” “turn it into sales copy,” or “help people want to try it.”
- **Input:** Required: an offer and its actual capabilities. Optional: target customers, problems, objections, evidence, channel, price, and desired action.
- **Method:** Open with a situation customers recognize. Describe a plausible desired outcome. Connect the solution to the product mechanism, add available supporting evidence, and finish with one action appropriate to the purchase stage.
- **Output:** One finished text suited to the channel and length constraint; provide alternative openings only when requested or useful for comparison.
- **Check:** Do not exaggerate fear, guarantee results, or add features. Ensure the body fulfills the opening and the action is possible with the supplied information.
- **Example:** “Turn this task reminder feature description into concise copy for business owners who chase updates through group chats.”

## M02 — Landing page messaging

- **Trigger:** “Write the landing page,” “the hero is unclear,” or “visitors do not understand the value.”
- **Input:** Required: a product, target users, and primary action. Optional: features, evidence, pricing, constraints, objections, and an existing page.
- **Method:** Structure the main promise, intended customer, benefit mechanism, workflow, proof, package limits, objection handling, and call to action. Follow visitors' questions rather than internal feature categories. Limit the scope when the user requests only a particular section.
- **Output:** Section copy with headings, body text, button labels, and relevant proof or visual requirements.
- **Check:** Do not invent testimonials, customer logos, prices, or guarantees. Put missing evidence in a separate note instead of presenting it as a finished claim.
- **Example:** “Write the hero and first three sections for an inventory app; the primary action is trying a demo without registration.”

## M03 — Answer an objection

- **Trigger:** “They say it costs too much,” “address this concern,” or “make the response more convincing.”
- **Input:** Required: the customer's objection and the offer under discussion. Optional: the full conversation, evidence, product limits, purchase stage, and alternatives.
- **Method:** Determine whether the objection relates to price, trust, need, timing, switching effort, or genuine mismatch. Acknowledge the reasonable concern, answer its substance with facts, and suggest a small step that helps the customer assess fit.
- **Output:** A ready-to-use response matching the relationship and channel; add a short strategic explanation only when useful to the user.
- **Check:** Do not shame customers, hide limitations, promise unauthorized discounts, or keep pushing after a clear refusal.
- **Example:** “A prospect says the app is too expensive for a four-person team. Write a short, reasonable WhatsApp reply.”

## M04 — Translate features into benefits

- **Trigger:** “This is too technical,” “sell the benefits,” or “why should anyone care about this feature?”
- **Input:** Required: a feature and its actual behavior. Optional: customer tasks, the previous workflow, limitations, and evidence of results.
- **Method:** Connect the feature to a change in activity, then to an outcome users experience. State conditions necessary for the benefit to apply. Choose the benefit most relevant to the segment rather than the most dramatic promise. Use measured evidence only when supplied or verified.
- **Output:** A feature-to-workflow-to-benefit table with supporting evidence; include finished copy when the intended result is promotional material.
- **Check:** Do not turn “automated” into “error-free” or “faster” into an invented percentage. Preserve capability limits.
- **Example:** “Explain the value of recurring tasks, attachments, and reminders to a retail operations manager.”

## M05 — Structure an offer

- **Trigger:** “Make the offer compelling,” “how should we package this?”, or “customers do not see the value.”
- **Input:** Required: a product or service, delivery capacity, and components that can actually be provided. Optional: prices, costs, segment, usage limits, policies, and buying patterns.
- **Method:** Specify the intended outcome, package contents, service limits, onboarding support, and purchase step. Introduce packages only when customer needs meaningfully differ. Label pricing or policy changes as proposals unless already approved.
- **Output:** A primary offer, clear inclusions and exclusions, a value rationale, and package options when appropriate.
- **Check:** Do not present invented guarantees, bonuses, discounts, or comparison values as official. Consider delivery costs so the offer can be fulfilled.
- **Example:** “Design software onboarding packages with clear inclusions, support limits, and differences between self-service and guided setup.”

## M06 — Sales email

- **Trigger:** “Write a promotional email,” “draft an event invitation,” or “make this email more appealing.”
- **Input:** Required: the message or offer and email objective. Optional: audience, recipient relationship, dates, links, sender identity, locale, and length limit.
- **Method:** Write a subject that matches the content, preview text that adds information, a relevant opening, primary benefits, necessary details, and one call to action. Use only known schedules and links. If essential information is missing, ask the blocking question or draft without asserting it.
- **Output:** Subject, preview text, and ready-to-copy body in the requested text or HTML format.
- **Check:** Do not invent dates, locations, free admission, personal relationships, or attendance numbers. Completing a draft does not authorize sending it.
- **Example:** “Draft an invitation to this free automation workshop using the supplied event details and registration link.”

## M07 — WhatsApp follow-up

- **Trigger:** “Follow up with this prospect,” “they have not replied,” or “continue this conversation professionally.”
- **Input:** Required: the conversation or a relationship summary and goal. Optional: time since the last reply, offer, promised follow-up, and preferred greeting.
- **Method:** Use the latest response to determine whether the exchange is open, paused, or finished. Choose one relevant reason: answering a question, supplying promised information, or suggesting a low-effort next step. Match the relationship's tone.
- **Output:** One concise ready-to-copy message, or a recommendation to wait when another message would add no value.
- **Check:** Do not invent familiarity, blame someone for not replying, or interpret acknowledgment as buying intent. A draft is not permission to send.
- **Example:** “They said they are in a meeting and will contact me later. Should I reply? If so, write one natural sentence.”

## M08 — Honest urgency and calls to action

- **Trigger:** “Strengthen the call to action,” “add urgency,” or “people are interested but do not act.”
- **Input:** Required: the desired action and offer. Optional: a genuine deadline, capacity, promotion limit, purchase stage, and action barriers.
- **Method:** Choose the smallest useful next action. Explain the immediate benefit and what happens after the click. Use urgency only when supported by a confirmed deadline, capacity limit, or other factual reason. Otherwise, improve relevance and clarity instead.
- **Output:** Three distinct calls to action and one recommendation; provide a single version when requested.
- **Check:** Do not invent countdowns, remaining places, price increases, or a final day. Ensure the destination fulfills the action's promise. Include timezone information when a deadline needs it.
- **Example:** “Improve the invitation to try this demo without inventing a promotion or scarcity.”

## M09 — Substantiate a differentiating claim

- **Trigger:** “Make this claim convincing,” “we are better than this alternative,” or “show our advantage over the current workflow.”
- **Input:** Required: the proposed distinction and comparison product or process. Optional: documentation, test results, package limits, and usage examples.
- **Method:** Break the claim into verifiable parts. Match each part to evidence and the conditions under which it holds. Compare equivalent tasks or capabilities. Replace unsupported superlatives with concrete distinctions that still communicate commercial value.
- **Output:** The finished claim, supporting evidence, applicability limits, and a narrower version when the evidence is insufficient.
- **Check:** Do not infer overall superiority from one feature. Do not present outdated competitor information as current. Keep material conditions visible.
- **Example:** “Replace ‘the fastest tool’ with a defensible differentiation claim based on these test results.”

## M10 — A/B messaging hypotheses

- **Trigger:** “Create versions to test,” “which copy performs better?”, or “design a messaging experiment.”
- **Input:** Required: a base message, audience, and target action. Optional: previous results, channel, audience size, duration, and distribution constraints.
- **Method:** Define one hypothesis, such as a time-saving benefit outperforming a control benefit. Create two versions that differ on that primary element while holding other elements stable. Select a goal-aligned metric, guardrail measures, and an evaluation rule before seeing results.
- **Output:** Two usable versions, the hypothesis, the changed element, and a measured way to interpret results.
- **Check:** Do not declare a winner before sufficient evidence. Do not change audience, offer, and copy together and attribute the result solely to wording.
- **Example:** “Test two ad openings: misplaced files versus missed deadlines. Keep the offer, audience, and call to action consistent.”
