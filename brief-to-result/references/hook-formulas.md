# 24 hook formulas: mechanisms, not canned sentences

Use with [Hook Lab](hook-lab.md). H IDs help authors and tests; users need not know them. Formulas describe the move to make, not exact wording to reproduce. Examples are original fictional illustrations, not tested ads or claims about a real product. The product's actual capabilities must support any adaptation. English is the base; preserve the intended effect rather than translating examples word for word.

## H01 — The tiny recurring interruption
- **Mechanism:** Recognition.
- **Formula:** A specific ordinary task interrupted by one small, repeated nuisance.
- **Use:** The product removes a concrete step or inconvenience.
- **Example:** "Phone needs charging. Cable's under the desk. Again."
- **Payoff:** Show the nuisance, then how the product changes that exact action.
- **Evidence:** Supported function; do not invent frequency, time saved, or universality.

## H02 — The inconvenient moment
- **Mechanism:** Recognition.
- **Formula:** A relevant moment when an otherwise tolerable problem becomes especially annoying.
- **Use:** Context gives the function meaning without catastrophic stakes.
- **Example:** "The call starts now. The webcam is pointing at your chin."
- **Payoff:** Show the setup adjustment and any compatibility requirement.
- **Evidence:** A plausible scenario, not a claim that this happened to the speaker.

## H03 — The unspoken workaround
- **Mechanism:** Recognition.
- **Formula:** Name an improvised habit the viewer may recognize, then question the extra step.
- **Use:** Users have normalized a clumsy workaround.
- **Example:** "Is that stack of cookbooks your phone stand?"
- **Payoff:** Demonstrate the actual alternative in the same setting.
- **Evidence:** Do not label the workaround unsafe or claim all users do it without support.

## H04 — The routine with one step removed
- **Mechanism:** Recognition.
- **Formula:** Show a familiar routine, then focus on the step the product can remove.
- **Use:** A feature changes an observable action.
- **Example:** "One less 'where did I put it?' before you leave."
- **Payoff:** Reveal the designated holder or supported retrieval mechanism.
- **Evidence:** Function-based claim only; no implied guarantee that users never lose anything.

## H05 — The expensive setup, awkward bottleneck
- **Mechanism:** Contrast.
- **Formula:** A valued setup contrasts with a modest unresolved inconvenience.
- **Use:** Accessories or workflows complement something users already own.
- **Example:** "A beautiful desk. A cable situation nobody wants to photograph."
- **Payoff:** Show the bottleneck and the relevant accessory in use.
- **Evidence:** No invented purchase price; joke about the setup, not the buyer's intelligence.

## H06 — Small part, concrete change
- **Mechanism:** Contrast.
- **Formula:** Contrast a modest object with the very specific action it changes.
- **Use:** A small physical detail produces a visible effect.
- **Example:** "This little stop is why the cable stays at the edge."
- **Payoff:** Show the physical mechanism close up.
- **Evidence:** Must hold in the shown conditions; avoid unsupported 'life-changing' promises.

## H07 — Replace the reflex, not the whole product
- **Mechanism:** Contrast.
- **Formula:** Question a common buying reflex, then name a smaller check worth making first.
- **Use:** The product addresses a narrow bottleneck, not every possible cause.
- **Example:** "Before buying another lamp, check where this one is pointing."
- **Payoff:** Demonstrate the relevant adjustment; admit other causes can remain.
- **Evidence:** No unsupported technical diagnosis or claim that an upgrade is never needed.

## H08 — The object as a comic character
- **Mechanism:** Contrast.
- **Formula:** Give a frustrating object one playful human behavior rooted in what it does.
- **Use:** Humor makes a mundane annoyance vivid.
- **Example:** "Your cable has a favorite place. Unfortunately, it's the floor."
- **Payoff:** Show the real cable movement and the holder's actual role.
- **Evidence:** Keep the exaggeration obviously figurative, not a false performance claim.

## H09 — The visual puzzle with a real answer
- **Mechanism:** Demonstration.
- **Formula:** Show an unusual detail and invite the viewer to notice its function.
- **Use:** The next shot genuinely resolves a visual question.
- **Example:** "That notch isn't decoration. Watch the cable."
- **Payoff:** Reveal what the notch does immediately.
- **Evidence:** The detail and function must exist; label proposed footage as a plan.

## H10 — The test-first opening
- **Mechanism:** Demonstration.
- **Formula:** Pose a narrow, observable test before making the verdict.
- **Use:** A demo can answer a useful product question.
- **Example:** "Does it hold when the desk moves? Let's check."
- **Payoff:** Show an honest test under stated conditions, including failure.
- **Evidence:** This is a test plan until performed; do not invent or preannounce a result.

## H11 — The overlooked function
- **Mechanism:** Demonstration.
- **Formula:** Point to one supported secondary use the viewer can see.
- **Use:** A real extra function matters to the audience.
- **Example:** "The lid has another job: keeping the scoop out of the flour."
- **Payoff:** Demonstrate the actual feature rather than vague 'multifunction' praise.
- **Evidence:** Do not invent extra functions or recommend unsafe unintended uses.

## H12 — The visible before-and-after action
- **Mechanism:** Demonstration.
- **Formula:** Contrast the same small task before and after one relevant change.
- **Use:** Physical organization or a workflow can be shown fairly.
- **Example:** "Same cables. Different place for the ends."
- **Payoff:** Show both conditions with a comparable view and real mechanism.
- **Evidence:** No fabricated footage or implied health, financial, or speed result.

## H13 — The fit filter
- **Mechanism:** Evaluation.
- **Formula:** State a specific use case that makes the product worth considering.
- **Use:** The right buyer matters more than broad hype.
- **Example:** "Desk against the wall? Start with the clearance, not the color."
- **Payoff:** Explain the relevant measurement or constraint.
- **Evidence:** Use supported dimensions; do not imply compatibility with every setup.

## H14 — Answer the real objection
- **Mechanism:** Evaluation.
- **Formula:** Voice one practical hesitation, then point toward a factual answer.
- **Use:** The audience already understands the category but doubts fit.
- **Example:** "Useful, sure. But will it fit your thicker cable?"
- **Payoff:** Show the supported size range or ask for the missing specification.
- **Evidence:** Do not imply the answer is yes before verifying it.

## H15 — Value for one job
- **Mechanism:** Evaluation.
- **Formula:** Anchor the buying decision to a concrete job, with a supported price only when known.
- **Use:** Affordability is relevant without fake premium equivalence.
- **Example:** "Buying it for this one job: keeping the cable within reach."
- **Payoff:** Demonstrate the job; add current price and conditions only if available.
- **Evidence:** Do not turn a low price into proof of equal quality or durability.

## H16 — One item instead of separate steps
- **Mechanism:** Evaluation.
- **Formula:** Name the supported functions or actions combined, not just a dramatic number.
- **Use:** Actual integration reduces switching or carrying.
- **Example:** "A phone stand that folds into the space beside your notebook."
- **Payoff:** Show the function, packed size, and limitations.
- **Evidence:** A multifunction count or replacement claim needs exact supported functions.

## H17 — A genuine change of mind
- **Mechanism:** Proof.
- **Formula:** The speaker's real initial doubt, then the concrete event that changed their view.
- **Use:** Actual creator review notes contain both the doubt and the experience.
- **Example:** "I doubted the clip until it held my cable through this desk move."
- **Payoff:** Share the real observation and conditions, not a universal guarantee.
- **Evidence:** Requires the speaker's genuine account. Without it, use H10 instead.

## H18 — A specific usage diary
- **Mechanism:** Proof.
- **Formula:** A real period of use plus one narrowly observed detail worth reporting.
- **Use:** Longitudinal review notes exist.
- **Example:** "Two weeks in, here's the one part I keep adjusting."
- **Payoff:** Explain the actual experience, including drawbacks where relevant.
- **Evidence:** Duration and observation must be supplied or verified; never fabricate ownership.

## H19 — Comparable A/B evidence
- **Mechanism:** Proof.
- **Formula:** One fair comparison condition and a specific difference the viewer can inspect.
- **Use:** Real footage, recordings, or measured outputs support comparison.
- **Example:** "Same room, same sentence. Listen to the two recordings."
- **Payoff:** Present comparable recordings and disclose meaningful setup differences.
- **Evidence:** Requires real comparable evidence; no invented test, competitor result, or metric.

## H20 — A documented buyer pattern
- **Mechanism:** Proof.
- **Formula:** A bounded, evidenced pattern in real buyer feedback points to one relevant detail.
- **Use:** Authentic reviews or adoption evidence support the actual pattern described.
- **Example:** "These reviews keep returning to one detail: the folding hinge."
- **Payoff:** Show the genuine feedback with its scope, then the actual feature.
- **Evidence:** No invented reviews, quote, crowd consensus, or 'everyone buys' leap. Retain relevant mixed feedback and disclose connections when applicable.

## H21 — Choose between two recognizable situations
- **Mechanism:** Participation.
- **Formula:** Present two concrete habits or setups, then connect the choice to a useful decision.
- **Use:** Audience participation helps select a relevant solution.
- **Example:** "Cable on the floor or tangled in the drawer—which is your desk?"
- **Payoff:** Show what the product does for the applicable setup.
- **Evidence:** Not a fake poll result; no need to require a comment before giving the answer.

## H22 — The viewer's next real use
- **Mechanism:** Participation.
- **Formula:** Name an upcoming or recurring use situation and a useful preparatory check.
- **Use:** Relevance provides a reason to act without artificial scarcity.
- **Example:** "Packing a desk kit? Try folding the stand before it goes in."
- **Payoff:** Show portability or setup constraints and a suitable next step.
- **Evidence:** Do not invent the viewer's schedule; frame it conditionally when unknown.

## H23 — The confirmed offer with a fit condition
- **Mechanism:** Participation.
- **Formula:** A genuine offer condition matters specifically to the buyer's intended use.
- **Use:** A current promotion, price, or deadline has actually been established.
- **Example:** "The two-pack offer matters if you need one at each desk."
- **Payoff:** Explain exactly what is included and the applicable terms before the CTA.
- **Evidence:** Requires confirmed offer facts. Without them, use H13 or H15; no invented deadline.

## H24 — The buyer's small discovery
- **Mechanism:** Participation.
- **Formula:** Invite the viewer to imagine a specific placement or use, not generic amazement.
- **Use:** A simple product has several legitimate contexts.
- **Example:** "Desk edge or bedside table—where would this clip earn its place?"
- **Payoff:** Demonstrate supported placements and surface restrictions.
- **Evidence:** Do not turn an imagined use into a real testimonial or unsupported compatibility.
